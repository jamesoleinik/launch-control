## Original expanded two-act arc (superseded for this cut)

### Act 1: Chat gives me the launch context

**Standalone opening:** The launch has a status in the business app, but the
decisions around it are scattered across work conversations and files.

1. Start with the launch record. Establish only its current, sourced business
   status.
2. Ask Chat to find related email and Teams conversations. Keep citations
   visible and exclude unrelated or personal material.
3. Ask Chat to find relevant files. Show their source and what they add.
4. Reconcile those sources with the live launch and task records. Identify
   conflicts, stale information, and gaps instead of smoothing them over.
5. Land the 360 view: a concise, source-backed account of the launch across
   productivity sources and current business status.

**Act 1 value:** I can see what people are saying and what the live launch
records say, with enough provenance to check both.

### Act 2: Cowork turns verified gaps into work

**Standalone opening:** I understand the launch, and now I need clear work
that people can own.

6. In a fresh Cowork task, retrieve the evidence independently and propose
   launch-linked tasks with role-based owners, due dates, completion signals,
   and any dependencies. Keep this read-only.
7. Review the exact task titles, launch links, assignees, and dates. Create
   tasks only after the presenter explicitly approves those exact changes.
   Read each saved task back from the business app.
8. Propose a monitoring condition, cadence, and follow-up. Schedule it only
   after the presenter explicitly approves its exact behavior and the
   capability is verified. Read the saved schedule back and safely verify
   what it will do.

**Act 2 value:** Approved work is linked to the launch, owned by people, and
supported by a monitor whose saved behavior is visible.

If task creation or scheduling is not supported or cannot be verified, do
not fake the result. End on the reviewable proposal and say plainly that the
write or schedule was not made.
