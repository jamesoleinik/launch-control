# Launch Control replacement voice-over

First person as the Launch Coordinator. Conversational transitions follow
the Summit written reference's problem, visible tool, and business-value
arc. Let the screen breathe during generation and reading. The synthetic
voice is a timing aid, not the presenter's final performance.

The native one-time run and its received Outlook email were read back and
captured. The briefing names its source records but has no clickable record
links, because the app did not return them.

## Set the stage (over the opening cards)

> I'm the Launch Coordinator for a fictional launch. Our saved status says
> we're at risk. I'll connect that record to the team's context, review
> follow-up and bring a useful briefing into my inbox.

## Script

**Act 1: From business context to follow-up.** Panel steps 1-4.

### 1. Set up the business context (0:00-2:26)

- **0:00** Before I ask about the launch, I check how business context reaches Copilot.
- **0:19** Here I can inspect the tenant's existing setting. I don't need to broaden access just to make this walkthrough.
- **0:32** Next I open the business-app tool in the catalog. Availability matters, but it isn't proof that a conversation can read my app.
- **1:20** I also check the environment where the records live. Under Product and Features, Work IQ is already on.
- **1:54** And I open the app's own settings to check Copilot. Now I can start with the business record rather than guess from a conversation.

### 2. Compare the launch with the team's evidence (2:26-4:23)

- **2:26** My team has several launches in flight. I open this one to check its saved risk and the evidence still under review.
- **2:43** The app gives me the business state. It doesn't tell me everything the team has been saying around it.
- **3:01** I ask Chat to compare the saved launch status and risk with the relevant email, Teams conversation and file. This review stays at launch level.
- **3:17** Chat starts with the recorded At Risk status and the certification review behind it.
- **3:27** The Teams conversation and certification brief add the team's context. Chat also separates the draft handoff from the later received briefing.
- **3:48** Those sources tell a consistent story. I can see how the team's context supports the saved risk without assembling that first comparison by hand.
- **4:08** And here are the sources behind the comparison. The business record and communications are together in one review.

### 3. Review follow-up with Cowork (4:23-6:12)

- **4:23** Understanding the issue is only the start. I also need to know what my team should follow up.
- **4:35** In Cowork I use the connected Business apps plugin and ask for a review based on recorded reasons, assignees and due dates.
- **5:09** This is still a review. I approve the scoped reads, not changes to the launch.
- **5:27** Here's the recorded blocker: partner security evidence is still under review. Cowork keeps it separate from a blocked task belonging to another launch.
- **5:38** The draft plan gives the role owner a concrete follow-up. I can review the proposed action alongside the recorded owner and due date.
- **5:54** The additional record read confirms that customer acceptance review hasn't started. It is not marked blocked.
- **6:03** I have a plan to review, and the business records are unchanged.

### 4. Receive the scheduled briefing (6:12-9:33)

- **6:12** I don't want the next readiness review to depend on remembering another prompt. So I set up a briefing for my own inbox.
- **6:32** The scope is deliberately narrow: this launch, read-only business data and one email to me. This demonstration runs once, not every day.
- **7:01** I ask for an executive summary and a readable table of the work that needs attention. Proposed next steps stay separate from saved facts.
- **7:21** I review the email permission. Only my own mailbox can receive this briefing.
- **7:48** I leave the immediate-run option off. The message should come from the scheduled run.
- **7:58** Here's the launch briefing in Automations. I can verify its one-time timing.
- **8:17** The timed run starts and asks me to approve its business data reads. I check their scope before it proceeds.
- **8:29** Cowork shows one completed run. Now I can check what reached my inbox.
- **8:41** Here is the briefing in Outlook, sent by Cowork. It separates saved facts from proposed work.
- **9:04** The table highlights blocked and overdue work, with role owners and due dates.
- **9:12** These recommendations give me next steps to review, not changes already made.
- **9:19** The review reaches me where I already work. I can make the next decision without starting another search.

## Keep it honest

- This is prepared fictional demo data, not customer records.
- The presenter approved enabling the intended existing Microsoft plugin
  and the one-time briefing. Duplicate plugins remain off.
- Portal loads, repeated reading pauses, and internal Cowork read approvals
  and lookup steps are edited for time. The narration makes no response-time
  claim. The new Chat pickup reviews the successful rehearsal's saved prompt,
  complete answer, and loaded Sources with real scrolling. Generation is
  not shown. An unsuccessful repeat is excluded, not spliced into that answer.
- No AI answer was rewritten. Privacy masks may conceal personal account
  details or unrelated material, not change business facts.
- Chat's revised prompt intentionally compares launch-level status and risk
  with communications. It does not request or claim task-level retrieval.
  The handoff is an unsent draft; the later readiness briefing is received.
- Cowork proposed business follow-up but did not change business records.
- The one-time scheduled run required attended Business apps read approvals.
  The presenter approved batching the remaining scoped reads. No blanket
  Do action permission was enabled. This is not unattended execution.
- The native run is completed and paused after its one-time trigger. Its
  email was received in Inbox, not Drafts. No manual or duplicate send was
  substituted. Direct record links were unavailable and are not claimed.
- The Summit comparison is against the bundled written script and style
  guide. No finished reference movie is supplied by that bundle.
